Session 4

Drone Flight

Controls, simulator practice, real flying and the rules

How a drone flies

  • Four motors spin four propellers
  • Neighbouring props spin in opposite directions, so their twisting forces cancel
  • All motors faster: the drone climbs
  • Some motors faster than others: the drone tilts and moves that way

CW and CCW

CW = clockwise: the same way the hands of a clock turn.

CCW = counterclockwise: the opposite way to a clock.

Understanding the controls

  • Left stick: throttle (up and down) and yaw (turn left and right)
  • Right stick: pitch (forward and back) and roll (left and right)
  • Throttle does not spring back: it stays where you leave it
  • Small, smooth stick movements work best

Four movements

Throttle

Go up or down

Yaw

Rotate left or right on the spot

Pitch

Move forward or backward

Roll

Slide left or right

Under each drone, the remote has two sticks: the orange dot shows the stick that moves, the other stays idle. Pitch is drawn from the side and roll from the front. Tip: until you are confident, keep the drone nose pointing away from you. When it faces you, left and right feel reversed.

Flight modes and safety switches

Arm and disarm

Arming starts the motors. Disarm right after landing, never in the air.

Stabilize or Angle

Self-levels when you release the sticks. Good first mode.

Altitude hold

Holds height by itself. Use it while you learn.

GPS hold or Loiter

Holds position in place, even in light wind.

Return to home

Flies back to the take-off point and lands. Set the return height first.

Failsafe

Triggers on signal loss or low battery. Know what your drone will do.

Real life: before take-off

  • Battery for drone and transmitter fully charged
  • Propellers tight, undamaged, on the right motors
  • Compass and GPS lock confirmed
  • Open area, light wind, no rain, no people close by
  • Permission checked on Digital Sky
  • Return height set above nearby obstacles

Real life: the flight

Flow

  • Arm, lift to about 2 m, hover and test controls
  • Fly slowly, keep the drone in sight
  • Return with spare battery, land, disarm

If something goes wrong

  • Low battery: land at once
  • Signal lost: let return to home work, do not panic
  • Out of control: lower throttle, keep people away

Toy and DIY drones: what is exempted

Model drones

Up to 25 kg, used only for educational, research, design, testing or recreation, and flown in sight. No type certificate needed.

Nano drones (up to 250 g)

No type certificate. No remote pilot licence for non-commercial flying. No UIN registration needed.

Still applies to everyone

Airspace zones, visual line of sight, 400 ft limit, no flying near airports or over people, and daylight flying.

Our DIY kit drones fall in this group when they meet these limits. Based on the Drone Rules, 2021. Confirm current rules at digitalsky.dgca.gov.in before flying.

Safe flying: the Do’s

  • Keep the drone in visual line of sight
  • Stay below 400 ft (120 m) in green zones
  • Check the Digital Sky airspace map first
  • Register and get your UIN where required
  • Fly in daylight and good weather
  • Respect privacy and local rules

Safe flying: the Don’ts

  • Fly near airports, borders or military sites
  • Fly over crowds, roads or people
  • Fly at night without permission
  • Fly in rain or strong wind
  • Carry or drop hazardous items
  • Fly after drinking or when tired
